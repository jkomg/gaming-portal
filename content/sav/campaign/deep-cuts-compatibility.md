# Can Deep Cuts work in Scum and Villainy?

**Yes, selectively.** Trauma is a strong first candidate; Harm can support a more character-driven space opera. Advancement can slow a long series. Load needs space-equipment exceptions. Downtime and Action require explicit design work because they touch Scum and Villainy's distinct economy, ship systems, and Gambits. None of these conversions is an official S&V supplement or a playtested claim.

This assessment uses the supplied **Blades in the Dark: Deep Cuts v1.1**, especially pp. 73–107, against the supplied S&V core and play sheets. “Deep Cuts” below means those published Blades rules. “Proposal” means our adaptation. The introductory job uses **core S&V throughout**. Keep Ghosts of the Wayline's existing choices separate.

## Module decisions

| Module | Deep Cuts change | S&V collision | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trauma, p. 76 | Player-defined immediate reaction; player invokes each condition once/session for XP | S&V already uses stress/trauma, but retirement and XP must be preserved | First isolated trial after the core job |
| Harm, pp. 73–75 | Fictional impediments and XP instead of fixed penalties; temporary level 1 harm | S&V has six-clock healing clearing all harm and medical abilities | Promising second trial; keep S&V medical economy |
| Load, p. 77 | Discreet 4, no heavy items; conspicuous 6, premark heavy | Spacesuits, medkits, small Urbots, and this cargo all use 2 load | Borrow presentation; keep core load limits and EVA access |
| Advancement, pp. 78–79 | Six-segment clocks with rising purchase costs | S&V has system/crew quality and CRED costs, not Tier/hold/turf | Optional slower campaign progression, separately priced |
| Downtime, pp. 80–90 | New payoff, vice, training, recovery, projects, upgrade and entanglement economy | Per-system Heat, upkeep, ship repair, Gambits, fixed-pay jobs, restricted extra activities | Retain core; selectively test diceless projects later |
| Action, pp. 91–107 | Threat Rolls, consequence assignment, merged push/resistance, Edge, changed teamwork | Gambits and many abilities depend on risky/desperate action rolls and resistance | Largest redesign; optional standalone prototype only |

## Trauma: smallest useful change

S&V and Blades share the necessary structure: stress track, trauma conditions, four-box retirement, and trouble-based XP. Deep Cuts changes who controls the manifestation and removes the automatic scene exit. That can keep a pilot at the controls during a climactic escape without making stress unlimited: overflow still creates a lasting condition, and the fourth still ends the character's scoundrel career.

Proposal: use the one-page overlay in [the playtest packet](/sav/wiki/deep-cuts-playtest). Only the player invokes a condition; each can award XP once per session. Replace only the trauma part of the end-session vice/trauma trigger. Vice trouble remains eligible normally. Do not pay XP twice for the same trauma invocation. A reaction need not involve cruelty, betrayal, or loss of control over the character. Long-term recovery is a negotiated project, not an automatically available cheap reset.

## Harm: preserve the science-fiction treatment rules

Deep Cuts removes fixed harm penalties in favor of the GM invoking a relevant limitation and awarding XP. Level 1 conditions can be numerous but temporary; repeated identical conditions become level 2 under its special rule. Taking stress equal to the harm level can prevent that injury being invoked again while it remains. Fatal harm that is not instantly lethal may be stabilized, leaving a scar.

S&V's own recovery is already comparatively quick: treatment clears level 1, and a completed **six-segment** clock clears **all harm**. Do not import Blades' different clock or level-by-level recovery underneath a supposedly lighter harm system. Proposal: keep S&V treatment rolls, Medical Bay +1d, Physicker, and other medical access. Use Deep Cuts only for injury descriptions, invocations, and toughing out. Cross out fixed penalties on the scratch sheet so they are not applied as well. Re-open a tough-it-out mark only after that injury heals and a new one is suffered, not automatically next scene.

This adds an XP source and may increase stress spending. Measure both before also slowing advancement. Mercy's Physicker remains a recovery bonus, not a second reduction to every injury. Unselected abilities such as Flesh Wound and Tenacious need interpretation against the new harm slots; do not assume a Blades ability with a similar name is identical. Our first trial excludes the stabilized-fatal-harm/scar option until its recovery procedure has been agreed.

## Load: don't make a spacesuit a genre penalty

Deep Cuts defines a heavy item as 2+ boxes. A literal “discreet cannot bring heavy items” means no standard spacesuit or medkit and no Pilot's fine small Urbot. Its swimming/climbing restrictions describe terrestrial burden rather than vacuum operations. The new 4/6 limits also change the S&V heavy 6–8 range; supplied sheets print 6.

Proposal: keep **3/5/6 for this starter**, preserve revealing gear, and declare obvious bulky equipment before an operation only as a presentation aid. Count picked-up cargo consistently: this case is 2 load alone, 1 each with two carriers, and ship cargo once stowed. A concealed suit under working clothes is a fictional question, not an automatic ban. This is a house convention inspired by Deep Cuts, not adoption of its Load module. If later testing full Load, explicitly rewrite Mule, rigging, suit access, medical gear, and ship resupply first.

## Advancement: specify what the clocks buy

Deep Cuts uses six-segment clocks, two clocks for a native special ability, three for Veteran, and one per crew-upgrade box. Raising an action costs the new rating, or the resulting attribute rating if higher. Attributes remain derived from actions with dots. S&V's normal cap is 3 action dots; importing four-dot Blades examples does not grant Mastery.

S&V core crew advances can buy **two upgrade boxes**, one system quality, one crew quality with its CRED payment, or an ability. Replacing “a crew advance” with “one clock” silently changes all those exchange rates.

If the group wants a slower campaign, our proposed prices are: native/crew ability 2 clocks; Veteran 3; upgrade 1 clock **per box**; system quality 2 clocks; crew quality 2 clocks **plus core 4 × new quality CRED**. Module slot limits, quality caps, fictional access, and recalculated upkeep still apply. Action increases use the larger of new action rating and resulting derived attribute rating, minimum 1 clock. Never buy an attribute independently. These system/crew-quality clock prices are our additions, not printed Deep Cuts.

Keep S&V training granting XP for a first advancement-only trial. Deep Cuts Downtime changes training into an activity that spends XP, which would be another simultaneous change. Record existing XP and agree a conversion at a session boundary; an easy pilot starts fresh trackers for a short alternate crew rather than altering an ongoing campaign's earned progress.

## Downtime: the least portable economy

Deep Cuts ties payout and Rep to target Tier, revises Heat/entanglements, adds a tithe/banking economy, prices upgrades with Coin, changes vice, and changes training and recovery. S&V instead has **negotiated CRED**, crew/system quality, ship upkeep, repair damage, and Wanted/Heat in four systems. It has no spendable Rep or crew turf/hold ladder. Its Heat threshold is 8. Its entanglement procedure uses one d6 in the local Wanted column. Lay low requires another system.

The Quiet Orbit's actual upkeep is **1 CRED** from its systems and crew quality; applying a Deep Cuts tithe on top is an untested extra drain. Paying each PC plus target Tier also invalidates the starter's negotiated 6-CRED contract. Ordinary S&V repair already has a fixed outcome: one activity and 1 CRED per damage, with Fuse's ability providing its stated exception. A wholesale diceless port would solve a problem repair does not have.

Recommendation: retain S&V payoff, capacities, stash/debt, upkeep, Heat, entanglements, recovery, vice, acquire asset, training, and ability allowances. To test less random progress later, use **one named long-term project** with predictable progress, chosen before work begins: 2 ticks for ordinary supported work, 3 with relevant specialist support, 1 with inadequate tools. These numbers are our trial rule, replacing only that project's progress roll. Do not use it for crafting quality, artifact research gates, ship repair, or recovery automatically. Compare completion time and lost bonuses with the core project, then decide whether to extend it.

The core vice roll can overindulge when it clears more stress than is marked. Deep Cuts' pay-one/clear-all/overindulge-at-six procedure changes both stress throughput and CRED pressure. Test it only as a separate economy experiment, with a written ruling for Mystic Gambit payments, Speaker allowances, and vice-related abilities.

## Action: feasible, but not a terminology swap

Deep Cuts usually assumes the described action happens and rolls for announced threats. Multiple threats add dice, with a distinct result assigned to each. A separate failure threat is needed when success itself is uncertain. Its new push roll replaces resistance and costs 0–3 stress; controlled threats need no roll; desperate rolls treat 1–5 as the threat. Teamwork and bargains change too.

S&V Gambits reward a risky 6 and buy bonus dice. Deep Cuts Edge also rewards sixes and can buy dice/effect. Using both unchanged would add two reward currencies while larger multi-threat pools generate more sixes. Abilities also care whether a roll was an action, desperate, a resistance, or a group action. Names shared with Blades are not proof the printed rewrite matches S&V.

Our [Threat Roll prototype](/sav/wiki/threat-roll-prototype) chooses **Gambits instead of Edge**, defines the trigger at resolution level, handles zero dice, and audits the selected abilities. It changes probability and spotlight distribution. It is untested, opt-in, and unsuitable as the first teaching session. Run the same small situation with core and prototype rules; compare stress, replenishment, number of rolls, and whether players can predict the stakes. An explicit fallback is better than quietly borrowing one paragraph from each system.

## Deeper setting cuts

The narrative material is more portable than the mechanical economy. Borrow the **kind of pressure**, not Doskvol names or geography: a struggle over created people's freedom, dangerous infrastructure, a contested religious explanation, or an exploitative industrial project. S&V already supplies Conclave 01, the Guilds, Cobalt Syndicate, the Church, Urbots, and Precursor mysteries. That makes a Conclave autonomy story a better fit than importing the Unchained as a duplicate faction.

Private follow-on opportunities are in the staff-only GM Preparation category. Players can instead decide which obligation or question they want next. A larger narrative should follow their choice; The cargo is not a compulsory chosen-one plot.

## Adoption order and evidence

1. Run the complete core starter; record stress, harm, Gambits, CRED, and downtime choices.
2. Ask whether trauma's scene exit, injury bookkeeping, or advancement speed actually caused a problem.
3. Trial Trauma alone for two sessions if desired. Add Harm only if the group wants its invocation style.
4. Choose a campaign advancement rate after seeing at least one ordinary advance. Keep ship costs explicit.
5. Trial Threat Rolls in a separate short encounter; retain core if stakes assignment or Gambit handling feels worse.

No actual table trial has been run for this packet. “Compatible” here means a reasoned design proposal supported by the supplied rules, not evidence of balance.

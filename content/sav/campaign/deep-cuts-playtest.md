# Deep Cuts: a small S&V playtest

**Inactive until the table chooses it. Original conversion; not an official or tested S&V supplement.** Use core rules for A Breathing Cargo. This packet begins after its debrief. Do not silently edit the pregens' printed trackers or switch procedures during a roll.

## Pilot A: Trauma only

Copy these five rulings onto a scratch card. Everything else stays core S&V.

1. **Overflow:** when a stress cost would overflow the nine-box track, mark one trauma box, choose a condition, and clear stress as the Deep Cuts reset. Resolve the triggering action; do not automatically remove the PC from the scene or cancel the escape they were piloting. For this trial the reset absorbs the remainder of that triggering cost; do not immediately re-charge it. This bookkeeping convention is ours.
2. **Reaction:** the player describes the immediate response and chooses its intensity. It can be withdrawal, detachment, anger, fear, or another appropriate response. Nobody requires betrayal, harm to another PC, or disclosure of personal experiences.
3. **Invocation:** only the player can invoke their trauma. Each condition can create a complication once per session for **1 XP**. Mark that use beside the condition. Ordinary portrayal without a complication is welcome but does not create unlimited XP.
4. **Session end:** keep specialty and belief/drive/heritage/background triggers. Keep the vice part of the vice/trauma trigger; do not also give an end-session award for trauma already paid through invocation. Clear the invocation-use marks for next session, not the conditions.
5. **Limits:** the fourth trauma box retires the PC. Before that, a recovery project may be negotiated with the GM; no default clock price guarantees removal. The overlay does not make trauma an obstacle the GM can force the player to invoke.

Deep Cuts p. 76 supplies the reaction, invocation, and retirement model. The absorbed-overflow convention and explicit S&V trigger bookkeeping are this packet's choices. Filling box nine exactly leaves nine stress; a further cost triggers overflow. Confirm this threshold before the pilot and use it consistently.

**Example:** Drift has 8 stress and accepts a 2-stress push while escaping. Record a trauma box, clear the track, and describe becoming emotionally distant while continuing to fly. If the player chooses to ignore a valued ally's request, creating a real problem, they may invoke the new condition and mark 1 XP. Merely choosing the word “Cold” does not itself earn XP. The GM cannot demand the ignored request.

## Optional later pilot: Harm

Use a separate session and preserve core six-clock healing. Level 1 conditions are temporary; level 2 has two slots and level 3 one. An identical repeated level 1 condition becomes level 2; apply the Deep Cuts exception that this repetition does not cascade into level 3. Other overflow follows the module. The GM may invoke a relevant harm to create a problem, reduce an applicable action/attribute, or worsen position/effect; the PC marks 1 XP. Replace the sheet's fixed level penalties, rather than applying both.

The player may suggest a different impediment or take stress equal to the level to tough it out. Cross out its invocation eligibility, not the injury; it still needs treatment. Temporary conditions clear through appropriate fiction, after a couple of invocations, or when downtime begins. Keep S&V medical access, clearing level 1 after treatment, Medical Bay and Physicker dice, and full healing on completing the six-clock. New harm still resets an incomplete clock. Leave stabilized fatal injury/scars out of this first harm trial; use the ordinary core fatal-harm stakes.

**Example:** Anchor has level 2 injured knee. GM invokes it to make a sprint across the gantry limited effect; Anchor marks 1 XP. Anchor can propose slowing down and using a rail, accept the limitation, or take 2 stress to tough it out. No extra automatic −1d is applied from the old sheet. The knee remains injured after toughing out.

Audit harm-modifying abilities before use, including Bodyguard (its harm-trigger stress recovery remains), and any newly selected Flesh Wound or Tenacious. Core armor still works against an incoming appropriate consequence. A harm invocation itself is not a new incoming wound to armor away.

## What remains unchanged

Gambits (reset 2; risky qualifying 6 earns 1), action and resistance rolls, group action leadership, all load limits, payoff, upkeep, per-system Heat/Wanted, entanglements, vice, CRED capacities, training, and crew advances. Two activities per PC when not at war. Silk's restricted activity and Fuse's extra repair remain intact. Six-segment recovery; no Deep Cuts tithe, banks, Rep, Tier/hold/turf, or automatic healing slowdown.

The advancement and predictable-project ideas in [the assessment](/sav/wiki/deep-cuts-compatibility) are separate experiments, not part of Pilot A. [The Threat Roll prototype](/sav/wiki/threat-roll-prototype) is another ruleset and must be explicitly selected separately.

Ask: Did players know what they were risking? Did anyone feel compelled to invoke a condition for XP? Did a change keep someone involved without making consequences irrelevant? Were specialist abilities still valuable? Keep the answer in the campaign session notes.

At a session boundary, agree to keep, revise, or revert. Retain earned XP and resources; record conversions explicitly rather than rolling back play outcomes. Returning to core restores scene-exit and end-session trauma rules for future incidents. Existing trauma and harm remain; remove pilot invocation marks. On a harm reversion, translate temporary conditions in the fiction and write injuries into core slots together before the next job. Never ambush a player with newly retroactive harm overflow.
